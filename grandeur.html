01 — Executive summary

Bridging the gap in tailored travel

Baby Boomers are underserved by a travel industry built around younger, more independent travelers. Grandeur's proposed solution is a personalized travel concierge service — run by Baby Boomers, for Baby Boomers — pairing visiting travelers with local hosts who know the destination and understand what this generation actually needs from a trip.

02 — Market understanding

A large, underserved, and well-resourced audience

The numbers make the opportunity concrete: this is a large population with real spending power and real time to travel — the market gap isn't a lack of demand, it's a lack of services built for how this generation actually wants to travel.

1 in 6

People in the U.S. were age 65+ in 2020, up from less than 1 in 20 in 1920.

$200B

Spent annually by the elderly on travel — a disproportionate share of leisure spending.

195B hrs

Leisure hours held by people 65+, looking for fun, stimulating, even transformative things to do.

03 — Target audience & needs

Safety, convenience, and personalization

Research into what this audience actually needs from a travel service converged on three priorities — all of which standard travel platforms, built for a younger and more tech-fluent traveler, tend to underserve.

04 — Solution overview

A peer-to-peer concierge model

Rather than a one-size-fits-all tour package, Grandeur connects a visiting Baby Boomer tourist with a local Baby Boomer acting as tour concierge — someone who hosts travelers passing through their town, while the tourist travels at ease knowing everything will be taken care of.

05 — Unique selling proposition

What sets Grandeur apart

Peer-to-Peer Concierge Service

Local Baby Boomer hosts, matched directly with visiting travelers of the same generation.

Tailored Transformative Experiences

Itineraries built around exploration and personal growth, not just sightseeing.

Holistic Well-being Focus

Safety, wellness, and accessibility considered as core service features, not afterthoughts.

06 — Design framework

Grounding the service in Maslow's hierarchy

To make sure the service addressed more than logistics, the design was mapped against Maslow's hierarchy of needs — starting from safety and moving up toward esteem and self-actualization, so the experience could support genuine personal growth, not just a checked-off itinerary.

07 — Service blueprint

Mapping the journey end to end

The blueprint traces the full journey — booking, the travel experience itself, and post-travel — layering in touchpoints like peer-to-peer matching, tailored itineraries, secure transportation, community events, and 24/7 concierge support, each tied back to a specific need on the Maslow framework.

08 — Business case

Sizing the opportunity and the price

The business case behind Grandeur: a large target market, a conservative capture rate, and a per-customer price point built up from real service cost components rather than picked out of the air.

Market penetration

The Baby Boomer travel market is estimated at $157 billion annually in the U.S. Capturing just 1% translates to roughly 343,000 travelers.

Price justification

Standard travel services range $100–$1,000+. Grandeur's $500 fee for a 3-day trip breaks down into itinerary planning, local concierge service, wellness and safety features, support, and marketing — projecting $171.5M in potential annual revenue.
